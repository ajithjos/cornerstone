# Adding and subtracting in columns

Put ones under ones, tens under tens and hundreds under hundreds. Put the sign just after the top number. Start with the ones.

## Add the ones first

```text
  3 4 +
    5
  ───
  3 9
```

Four ones and five ones make nine ones. The three tens stay. **34 + 5 = 39.**

## When the ones add up to 16

```text
  1
  2 8 +
  1 8
  ───
  4 6
```

Eight plus eight makes sixteen. Write **6** under the ones. Carry **1 ten** above the tens. Now add the tens: 2 + 1 + 1 = 4. **28 + 18 = 46.**

## Subtract by trading a ten

```text
    7
  9 8 7 −
  1 2 8
  ─────
  8 5 9
```

In **987 − 128**, the ones need more. Trade one of the eight tens for ten ones. Write **7** above the 8 to show the seven tens left.

- Ones: 17 − 8 = 9.
- Tens: 7 − 2 = 5.
- Hundreds: 9 − 1 = 8.

## Write 7 and think 17

```text
  8 7
  9 8 7 −
  1 9 8
  ─────
  7 8 9
```

In **987 − 198**, make the same first trade. Write **7** above the 8. The ones answer is 9.

Now you have seven tens and need to take away nine tens. Trade one hundred for ten more tens. Write **8** above the 9 hundreds.

**Keep the 7 as your working mark and think “seventeen tens.”** You can also write **17** in that same box. Either way, 17 − 9 = **8** goes in the tens answer box. Finally, 8 − 1 = 7 hundreds. **987 − 198 = 789.**

With four digits, each mark still belongs to its own column. In **3,772 − 1,888**, both middle columns give one away and get ten more. Write **6** above each 7 and think **16** in each of those columns. The answer is **1,884**.

## Trade across a zero

```text
  4 9
  5 0 3 −
  2 6 7
  ─────
  2 3 6
```

In **503 − 267**, the tens start at zero. Trade one hundred for ten tens. Pass one of those tens to the ones. You now have **4 hundreds, 9 tens and 13 ones**. Subtract from the right: 13 − 7 = 6, 9 − 6 = 3, and 4 − 2 = 2. Check by adding: **236 + 267 = 503**.

# Written Division Lesson Note

Written division keeps the sharing or grouping organised by place value.

The answer is called the quotient. Anything left over is the remainder.

The remainder must be smaller than the divisor.

## Short division

Use short division when the divisor has one digit and the facts are familiar.

Example: `864 / 4`

```text
4 ) 864
```

1. Hundreds: `8 hundreds / 4 = 2` hundreds.
2. Tens: `6 tens / 4 = 1` ten with `2` tens left.
3. Exchange the `2` tens into `20` ones, so the ones work is `24 / 4`.
4. Ones: `24 / 4 = 6` ones.

So:

```text
864 / 4 = 216
```

Check:

```text
216 x 4 = 864
```

## Long division

Use long division when the divisor has more than one digit or when the steps need more space.

Example: `945 / 27`

Ask how many `27`s fit into `94`.

```text
27 x 3 = 81
94 - 81 = 13
```

Bring down the `5` to make `135`.

```text
27 x 5 = 135
135 - 135 = 0
```

So:

```text
945 / 27 = 35
```

Check:

```text
35 x 27 = 945
```

## Division with a remainder

Example: `587 / 24`

```text
24 x 24 = 576
587 - 576 = 11
```

So:

```text
587 / 24 = 24 remainder 11
```

Check:

```text
24 x 24 + 11 = 587
```

The remainder `11` is smaller than the divisor `24`, so the answer is in a valid remainder form.

## Good learner self-talk

- `Which part of the dividend am I dividing now?`
- `What is the largest sensible quotient digit?`
- `Did I multiply, subtract, and bring down in order?`
- `Is the remainder smaller than the divisor?`
- `Can I check with divisor times quotient plus remainder?`

## Quick spoken practice

1. Why must a remainder be smaller than the divisor?
2. How do you check `945 / 27 = 35`?
3. In long division, what comes after multiplying by the quotient digit?
